The Extended Metcalfe Theorem for Agentic Systems
Metcalfe (1983):   V = n²
Reed's Law (1999):   V = 2ⁿ   (group-forming networks)
Agentic Law:       V = n² × I(t) × e^(λt)
n = network nodes (providers / clients) I(t) = intelligence function (grows with learned patterns) e^(λt) = compounding exponent (λ ≈ 0.04/mo empirically) Switching Cost = Time × Learning Rate × Network Size
Kleiber's Law says biological metabolic rate scales at n^(3/4) — organisms get more efficient as they grow larger, but eventually plateau. Agentic networks beat biology: intelligence scales at n². The deeper insight: biological systems lose information at death. Agentic systems *accumulate* it. This is the first time in recorded history that institutional memory has no theoretical ceiling.
